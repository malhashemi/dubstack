# Visual and interaction patterns

Use these as possibilities to think with. The input and the user's next action determine the composition; no row implies a required layout, control set, or content checklist.

| What should become clear? | A possible visual | Useful interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What changes? | Aligned before/after views, an overlay, or a structural diff | Switch alternatives or annotate the changed region. |
| How does it unfold or change? | An animated SVG of a journey, a process, data in motion, or a before turning into an after | Play, pause, or step through it, and replay the moment that matters. |
| How do things relate? | A Mermaid graph, SVG map, nested boxes, or a sequence | Trace one path, focus a group, or comment on a node or connection. |
| What is hidden by the current structure? | Cross-sections, stacked bands, surface/depth rectangles, or a collapsed tree | Expand internals or move between overview and detail. |
| What happens if something changes? | A live simulation, chart, or preview beside meaningful inputs | Change an assumption and see the consequence in place; compare useful scenarios. |
| Which option fits? | Aligned alternatives or a tradeoff plot with the deciding evidence nearby | Compare a criterion, select an option, or record the reason for a choice. |
| What needs attention? | An annotated document, diagram, timeline, or change view | Accept, reject, question, or comment on a specific item, then collect the response. |
| Where is understanding incomplete? | A concept map, flow sketch, pseudocode, or a shallow responsibility tree | Mark an unclear relationship and turn it into a focused question. |

The visual itself can be the control: selecting a region, tracing a path, or marking an uncertain connection may be more direct than a separate panel of settings. Use supporting controls when they help, and keep the explanation legible before any interaction.

For example, a selected alternative plus a comment can become “Use option B for order intake, but keep validation visible because operators need to diagnose rejected orders.” The subject, choice, and reason survive outside the visualization.
