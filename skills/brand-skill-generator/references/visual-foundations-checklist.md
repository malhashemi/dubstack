# Visual Foundations

Account for each dimension below through usable guidance, a reason it does not apply, or an explicit deferral. Derive the detail from intended applications and existing evidence. A print-only identity can mark interface states inapplicable; a software brand cannot treat them as optional simply because a marketing preview looks finished.

Use values, actual assets, examples, and reasons where they change a future decision. These are coverage requirements, not a demand to use every effect or follow a universal aesthetic. Group the resulting guidance around how this brand will be used.

## Recognition and composition

| Dimension | Decisions the system needs |
|---|---|
| Organizing expression | The recognizable relationship among language, marks, typography, color, imagery, and composition; why it fits the brand |
| Color | Exact accepted anchors, generated ramps when needed, primary/neutral/semantic roles, emphasis and area of use, foreground/background pairings, themes, and print treatment |
| Typography | Faces and roles, hierarchy, size or scale, weights and case, leading, tracking, line length, reading versus display needs, fallback and language support |
| Composition | Alignment, grids or other organizing principles, hierarchy, proportions, whitespace, cropping, and adaptation to different formats |
| Spacing and density | A usable rhythm and scale, compact versus expressive applications, grouping, and clear space |
| Backgrounds and surfaces | Flat fields, full-bleed imagery, patterns, textures, gradients, or other treatments; where and why each belongs |
| Marks and signatures | Canonical mark variants, clear space, reproduction limits, recurring graphic devices or material treatments, and when they carry recognition |

Follow [Color craft](color-craft.md), [Type craft](type-craft.md), and [Logo craft](logo-craft.md) for their specialized decisions. Preserve accepted anchors and source assets; keep proposals distinguishable from faithful extraction.

## Shape, layers, and behavior

- **Borders and dividers:** weight, color, placement, and their role in grouping or separation.
- **Corners and containers:** radius rules, exceptions by scale or component, and how cards or equivalent groupings use fill, border, shadow, and spacing. Do not introduce cards merely to demonstrate a token.
- **Shadows and elevation:** whether depth exists, what it communicates, usable levels, and when an inset or outer treatment is appropriate.
- **Transparency and blur:** where translucency belongs and how content remains legible over changing backgrounds; an explicit absence can be a valid decision.
- **Interactive states:** hover, press, focus, selection, disabled, loading, success, and error behavior where applicable. Define visible changes and recovery; cover keyboard and non-color cues rather than treating a hover effect as the interaction system.
- **Motion:** its purpose, characteristic movement, pace, duration, easing, transitions, and reduced-motion behavior. Distinguish meaningful behavior from ornamental repetition.

Connect these choices to the brand and the user's task. “Calm” needs a visible consequence; “no shadows” is useful when paired with how separation and hierarchy are achieved instead.

## Imagery, icons, and time-based media

- **Imagery:** subjects, perspective, composition, lighting, color treatment, texture, realism or abstraction, and relationship to typography and marks. Include selection or creation examples specific enough to guide a search, photography brief, or image generation request.
- **Sourcing:** real product or place assets when truth matters, licensed sources, or generated imagery when appropriate to the assignment. Record provenance and useful constraints. Use actual verified assets in applications that depend on imagery; label conceptual work and missing material accurately. A guessed URL or colored placeholder is not a completed image treatment.
- **Iconography:** family or construction principles, stroke versus fill, weight, optical size, corner behavior, relationship to typography, and use of emoji or other symbols. Specify accessible labeling at the use site.
- **Video and animation:** when intended, define framing, sequence, pacing, transitions, title and caption treatment, and how identity holds over time. Derive them from the brand rather than merely animating a static layout.
- **Sound and spoken performance:** when intended, describe voice, delivery, music or sonic character, and boundaries. Provide usable guidance or an explicit development need; a written tone adjective alone does not establish an audio identity.

For existing signature elements, preserve the source and explain its behavior and adaptations. When only a screenshot or flattened asset exists, distinguish what can be observed from what must be inferred or reconstructed.

## Adaptation and verification

Explain what stays recognizable across media and what can change with density, format, language, audience, accessibility, or production. Identify the actual constraints and enough rationale to handle an application not yet illustrated. A component specimen demonstrates one solution; it does not establish a universal page layout.

Check that the direction follows the brand's intent rather than a reflexive category style or a fashionable inversion of it. Familiarity can be useful; distinctiveness should come from specific, coherent choices rather than novelty for its own sake.

Inspect representative applications at actual reading sizes, backgrounds, and reproduction conditions. Verify loaded fonts, marks, image treatment, hierarchy, contrast, and relevant states together. A list of tokens is usable material, but the rendered relationships establish whether the identity works.
