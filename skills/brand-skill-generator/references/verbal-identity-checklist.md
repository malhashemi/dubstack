# Verbal Identity

Use this checklist to make strategy usable in language. Account for each dimension in the guidelines through a concrete decision, a reason it does not apply, or an explicit deferral. Resolve it from evidence when possible; bring meaningful factual gaps and creative choices into the interview. The checklist governs coverage, not the order of the conversation or the headings of the output.

## Messaging: what the brand can credibly say

- **Value proposition:** state the central promise to the priority audience in a concise, reusable sentence. Identify what is valuable and deliverable. If the claim fits any competitor equally well, sharpen it with the brand's actual capabilities or perspective rather than inventing exclusivity.
- **Supporting themes:** develop the messages needed to explain the promise. Connect each audience need to an outcome, a real capability, and usable example language. Choose the number from the substance rather than filling a fixed pillar count.
- **Proof and qualification:** give each substantive claim an evidential basis: a demonstrated capability, example, documented outcome, or other support. Preserve the source and any limitation that affects publication. Mark an unsupported aspiration as such; do not let polished copy turn it into a fact.
- **Reusable descriptions:** provide a one-line description, a compact introduction, and a fuller explanation suited to the actual channels, such as a bio, listing, document introduction, or press description. Adapt lengths to use and consolidate genuinely redundant forms. These should be ready to reuse, not instructions to improvise the messaging again.
- **Spoken explanation:** capture how a person explains the offering naturally in conversation. Use it to check whether the promise remains clear outside website copy; record when a distinct spoken form is unnecessary.
- **Tagline:** preserve or develop one only when appropriate. If present, record its role, placement, and limitations; a tagline does not replace the value proposition.
- **Audience emphasis:** when the brand serves different audiences, show how the same underlying promise changes in emphasis and proof for each. Resolve contradictory promises rather than hiding them in separate variants.

Separate approved wording, proposed wording, verified facts, and ambitions. An established brand may already supply most of these answers; preserve its distinctive language rather than rewriting it into a generic framework.

## Voice: recognizable behavior

Translate personality into writing decisions with reasons:

- **Tone and posture:** how the brand relates to the reader, expresses confidence, handles uncertainty, and earns trust.
- **Person and directness:** use of I, we, you, or impersonal constructions, including who is speaking on different surfaces.
- **Energy:** calm, exuberant, restrained, playful, technical, or another grounded quality; show its effect on pace, emphasis, humor, and intensity.
- **Expression:** vocabulary, sentence shape, detail, rhythm, and degree of formality. Identify the habits that distinguish this brand and the boundaries that prevent caricature.

For example, “quietly confident” could mean stating a supported capability directly and acknowledging its limits. The phrase alone cannot guide a writer. Include contrasting examples and explain the meaningful difference.

## Registers: one voice in different circumstances

Account for these contexts and add others demanded by the brand. Establish the reader's task, emotional situation, useful degree of expression, and appropriate information density. Mark a context inapplicable when it has no intended use.

| Context | Decisions to resolve |
|---|---|
| Marketing and brand expression | How the promise, benefits, proof, and invitation work together; expressive range without unsupported claims |
| Product and service interfaces | Labels, actions, empty states, confirmations, and recovery; clarity under limited space and attention |
| Documentation and instruction | Terminology, assumed knowledge, explanation depth, sequence, and precision for someone completing a task |
| Support, errors, and sensitive moments | Acknowledgment, responsibility, what happened, and what to do next; appropriate empathy without blame or forced humor |
| Physical, spoken, or other touchpoints | Adaptation to the human interaction, available space, duration, and circumstances |

Supply usable on-brand examples and contrasting off-brand examples for each applicable register. Show why the contrast matters and preserve the same underlying facts so the difference teaches voice. Include ordinary utility language as well as the most expressive brand copy. Test whether the brand remains recognizable when its logo and name are absent.

## Terminology, naming, and mechanics

- **Preferred language:** domain and product terms, their meanings and casing, and alternatives that would introduce ambiguity. Keep the same concept named consistently across surfaces.
- **Excluded language:** misleading claims, category clichés, incompatible metaphors, or habits that violate this brand's posture. Give the reason; avoid a generic blacklist detached from the identity.
- **Naming:** how future products, features, components, services, and releases join the existing vocabulary. Record useful patterns and their limits without inventing naming rules for nonexistent product plans.
- **Mechanics:** headline and body casing, punctuation, emoji, numbers, dates, units, and other choices where consistency affects the voice or usability. Defer to normal language conventions where the brand has no special requirement.
- **Languages and localization:** supported languages, translation of the promise, protected names, formality, and cultural adaptations. Specify where native-language review or further work is needed rather than claiming an untranslated example covers every locale.

## Check the result in use

Use the language in representative applications alongside the visual system. A list of personality adjectives, empty messaging headings, or copy that relies on invented proof does not establish verbal identity. The result should let another agent write a new piece, choose the appropriate register, and explain why it belongs to this brand.
