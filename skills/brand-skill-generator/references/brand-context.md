# Brand Context

Inputs establish both what the brand is and what the user wants to change. Their format does not determine their authority. Begin with the assignment and existing brand-skill discovery, then interpret the supplied evidence against that intent.

## Intake across sources

Gather the material available in the conversation and relevant project: briefs, guidelines, rendered work, code and tokens, marks and fonts, writing, imagery, documents, and references. Inspect what each can actually establish. Source code reveals implementation choices; rendered work reveals their effect; neither necessarily explains why those choices were made.

Establish the following from that material before asking for missing information:

- Offering, priority audience, their situation and alternatives, and the outcomes that matter to them.
- Positioning, functional and emotional value, promise, and evidence supporting publishable claims.
- Personality expressed as behavior, desired audience perception, and references or counterexamples with the aspects that matter.
- Intended applications and their relative importance, languages, accessibility needs, distribution, and production constraints.
- Settled names, colors, typography, marks, verbal conventions, signature treatments, and assets the user wants preserved.
- What the user considers successful, unsatisfactory, negotiable, or unknown in existing work, and how much creative judgment they have delegated.

For a fictional company, the brief can establish invented premises. For a real company, distinguish factual claims from aspirations and proposed positioning. Do not invent adoption, outcomes, certifications, or proof to complete a framework.

## Decide how to use existing work

Assess authority per area rather than assigning one mode to the entire project:

| Evidence and intent | Useful approach |
|---|---|
| The user considers the existing identity authoritative | Extract and codify it; fill explanatory gaps without introducing a redesign |
| The user says the implementation misses their intended brand | Audit where it succeeds and fails against that intent; preserve useful work and propose improvements |
| Some elements are settled and others are open | Hold the settled elements fixed while developing the unresolved parts |
| Little identity exists beyond a brief or references | Develop a coherent identity, using the brief as premises and references for the specific qualities they demonstrate |

Current explicit user direction governs conflicts. Approved guidelines are stronger evidence of intended identity than incidental implementation defaults. When materials disagree and the difference would change the outcome, explain the conflict and seek the missing decision. Avoid making the user classify every extracted value.

When examining a codebase, `scripts/extract-style-candidates.py` can inventory recurring values and assets. Counts are leads, not votes for what the brand should become. Trace important candidates to their rendered use, semantic role, and source. Distinguish canonical resources, useful patterns, local exceptions, and obsolete or accidental choices. Preserve exact accepted assets and contracts; do not canonize accidental inconsistencies.

An audit should explain the useful difference between the existing expression and the intended one. Tie proposed changes to that gap and demonstrate the effect. A general desire for refinement is not evidence that every element needs replacing. Preserve original source assets during exploration and record accepted deviations in the new skill; changing the running product remains a separate scope decision.

## Establish a direction that can guide choices

Connect strategy to expression: what should the audience recognize, feel, or be able to do? Translate personality into consequences for language, composition, imagery, and behavior. A material, service behavior, cultural context, or existing motif may organize the system; an invented metaphor is not mandatory.

Judge direction by its fit, coherence, specificity, and ability to support the intended range of work. Familiar conventions can serve recognition and usability. Explain why they fit instead of equating novelty with quality. Test creative hypotheses through comparable applications in [the interview](interview.md).

Name the qualities that should persist across media and those that adapt to the audience, task, and production conditions. This is the bridge from identity to application: it helps future agents make new work without imitating one example's layout.

## Name and destination

Keep an established name unless reconsideration is requested. Develop enough context to support naming when it is open; distinguish a working name from a settled one. Do not claim legal, domain, or commercial availability without evidence.

Continue the interview while naming or the final kit destination is unresolved. Settle the name or an authorized working name before packaging it into the skill name and path. Infer the destination from the project and request when possible; ask only when the choice matters and remains unclear.
